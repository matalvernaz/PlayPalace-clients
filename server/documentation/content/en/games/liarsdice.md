\*\*Liar's Dice\*\*

Liar's Dice is a bluffing dice game for 2 to 6 players. Everyone rolls their dice in secret, then players take turns claiming how many dice of one face are on the whole table. Each claim must top the last one, until someone calls the last claim a lie. Whoever is wrong loses dice. Lose all your dice and you are out; the last player with dice wins.

\*\*Rounds\*\*

Each player starts with 5 dice unless the host changes it. At the start of every round, everyone still in rolls all of their dice. The game announces the round number and the total number of dice on the table, and tells each player privately which dice they rolled, lowest first, and how many of each face they have.

Players take turns in seating order, skipping anyone who is out. The first player seated opens the first round.

\*\*Bidding\*\*

A bid names a quantity and a face, such as "three fours". It means: among all the dice on the table, counting everyone's, at least three show a four.

On your turn, either make a bid or challenge the last one. The first bid of a round has no minimum. After that, every bid must be higher than the one before:

\* \*\*Higher quantity:\*\* More dice of any face. After "three fives", "four twos" is a legal bid.

\* \*\*Same quantity, higher face:\*\* After "three fives", "three sixes" is a legal bid.

When 1s are wild, bids on ones follow the special rules below instead.

\*\*Make a bid\*\* opens a list of legal bids, starting with ones and working up through the faces, smallest quantity first. The list holds at most 60 bids, so with more than 10 dice on the table it can end before the higher faces.

\*\*Wild Ones\*\*

When 1s are wild, which is the default, every 1 on the table also counts toward a bid on twos through sixes. A bid on ones counts only the ones themselves. Ones then follow their own bidding rules:

\* \*\*Switching to ones:\*\* You need at least half the current quantity, rounded up. After "five fours", the lowest bid on ones is "three ones".

\* \*\*Raising ones:\*\* After a bid on ones, another bid on ones needs a higher quantity.

\* \*\*Switching back from ones:\*\* You need more than double: twice the quantity plus one. After "three ones", you need at least seven of any other face.

When 1s are not wild, ones are simply the lowest face and follow the normal rules.

\*\*Calling\*\*

Instead of bidding, you can challenge the bid just made:

\* \*\*Call Liar:\*\* You say the bid is too high. All dice are revealed and counted. If there are at least as many as the bid named, the bid was honest and you lose a die. If there are fewer, the bidder loses a die.

\* \*\*Call Spot On:\*\* You say the bid is exactly right. If the count matches the bid exactly, every other player still in loses a die. If it doesn't, you lose two dice. This call is only offered when the Spot On option is on.

After a call, a new round begins. It is opened by the player who lost a die to Call Liar, or by the caller after any Spot On call. If that player is out, the next player still in after them opens instead.

A player with no dice left is eliminated. When only one player has dice, that player wins.

\*\*Customizable Options\*\*

\* \*\*Starting dice per player:\*\* How many dice each player starts with (default 5, range 3 to 8).

\* \*\*1s are wild:\*\* Whether ones count toward bids on other faces (default on).

\* \*\*Spot On call enabled:\*\* Whether players may call Spot On as well as Liar (default on).

\*\*Keyboard Shortcuts\*\*

\* \*\*B:\*\* Make a bid.

\* \*\*L:\*\* Call Liar.

\* \*\*S:\*\* Check status: the round, your dice and how many of each face you have, the current bid, and the total dice on the table.

\* \*\*Shift+S:\*\* Detailed status: how many dice each player has left, and who is out.

\* \*\*T:\*\* Check whose turn it is.

Call Spot On has no shortcut; choose it from your turn menu.

\*\*Strategy\*\*

\* Start from what you know: your own dice. With wild ones, each die you can't see has a 1 in 3 chance of counting toward a bid on twos through sixes, because it may show that face or a one. For a bid on ones, or for any face without wild ones, the chance is 1 in 6.

\* So with wild ones, a fair guess for twos through sixes is your own dice that count, plus a third of everyone else's dice. A bid well above that is a good target for Call Liar.

\* Spot On is a long shot. It can cost every other player a die, but you lose two dice when it misses.
