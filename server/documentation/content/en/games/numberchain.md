\*\*Number Chain\*\*

Number Chain is a two-player puzzle duel on a grid of 4 rows and 8 columns. Together, the two players lay one long chain of numbered tiles, counting 1, 2, 3 and so on up to 8, then starting again at 1. Every tile must touch the one placed just before it. The goal is to leave your opponent with no legal place to put the next number.

\*\*Setup\*\*

Number Chain is for exactly 2 players. At the start, the game randomly names one player as player 1 and the other as player 2. Player 1 goes first.

Each player holds two tiles of every number from 1 to 8, so 16 tiles each.

\*\*Your Turn\*\*

You don't choose which number to place: it is always the next number in the chain, taken from your own tiles. You only choose where it goes.

\* \*\*The first tile:\*\* The opening 1 can go on any square.

\* \*\*Every tile after that:\*\* It must go on an empty square directly above, below, left or right of the tile placed just before it, whoever placed that tile. Diagonal squares don't count.

After an 8, the chain starts again at 1.

\*\*How the Game Ends\*\*

When your turn comes and you can't place a tile, you lose, and the player who placed the last tile wins. That happens when every square next to the last tile is already full, or when you have no tile left with the number the chain needs.

Because turns always alternate, player 1 places every odd number (1, 3, 5 and 7) and player 2 places every even number (2, 4, 6 and 8). Each player therefore only ever uses half of their tiles. If the chain runs from 1 to 8 twice, filling 16 squares, player 1 needs a third 1, has none left, and loses. Player 1 must trap player 2 before that happens.

\*\*The Board\*\*

The board is a grid of 32 squares. Use the arrow keys to move around it and press Enter to place your tile on the square you are on. Each square is read as its row and column, then either "empty" or the number on its tile and whose tile it is, such as "Row 2, column 5, 3, yours". Your tiles and your opponent's tiles also sound different as you move over them. If you choose a square where the tile can't go, you hear "That move is not legal."

\*\*Customizable Options\*\*

\* \*\*Bot difficulty:\*\* How bots choose their squares (default Simple; choices Random or Simple). Random bots pick any legal square. Simple bots take a square that leaves you no reply whenever there is one, and otherwise the square that leaves you the fewest replies.

\*\*Keyboard Shortcuts\*\*

\* \*\*Arrow keys:\*\* Move around the board.

\* \*\*Enter:\*\* Place your tile on the current square.

\* \*\*N:\*\* Hear the next number to be placed.

\* \*\*E:\*\* Hear whose turn it is and the next number.

\* \*\*I:\*\* Hear how many tiles of each number you have left.

\* \*\*T:\*\* Check whose turn it is.

\*\*Strategy\*\*

\* The square you choose decides where your opponent must play next. A tile with no empty square beside it wins the game on the spot.

\* Corner squares have only two neighbors and edge squares three, so tiles in corners, along edges or next to filled squares make the tightest traps.

\* Before you place a tile, consider what your opponent could do from that square. Avoid giving them a square from which every reply leaves you trapped.

\* As player 2, you win by surviving: if the chain fills 16 squares, the win is yours.
