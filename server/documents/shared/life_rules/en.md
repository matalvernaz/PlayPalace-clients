# The Game of Life Rules

The default PlayPalace game follows Milton Bradley's 1991 classic edition for
2 to 6 players. Lobby settings identified below as variants deliberately alter
those rules.

## Object and Setup

Finish with the highest combined value of cash and LIFE tiles.

Each player starts with $10,000. Four of the 25 LIFE tiles are placed at
Millionaire Estates; the other 21 form a face-down draw pile. Career, Salary,
House Deed, Stock, insurance, and bank-loan cards begin in their respective
pools.

Every player spins once. Highest spin acts first, and play continues clockwise.

## Starting a Career or College

At the start of your first turn, choose a path and then spin during that same
turn.

Start Career:

- Receive one random non-degree Career Card.
- Receive one random Salary Card.
- Join the main route after the college section.

Start College:

- Take two $20,000 bank-loan notes to cover $40,000 tuition.
- Follow the college route.
- Stop at Job Search even if your spin would carry you farther.
- Draw 3 Career Cards and choose 1, then draw 3 Salary Cards and choose 1.
- Spin again after Job Search.

Teacher, Doctor, and Accountant require a college degree.

## Turns, Movement, and Stops

Spin 1 through 10 and move that many spaces. If an ordinary destination is
occupied, continue to the next open space.

Orange spaces are mandatory: follow their directions. Blue spaces are
optional. Green Pay Day spaces pay your Salary Card whenever you pass or land
on them, including multiple Pay Days in one move.

Red Job Search, Get Married, and Buy a House spaces stop movement immediately,
regardless of the spin. After resolving one, spin again.

## Career and Salary Rules

Career spaces name a Career Card. Pay the printed amount to the player holding
that career. If nobody holds it, pay the bank. A player landing on their own
career space pays nothing.

On Taxes Due, pay the amount on your Salary Card to the Accountant. If nobody
is the Accountant, pay the bank. The Accountant pays nothing on their own
Taxes Due.

When fired or required to change careers, return the old Career and Salary
Cards and draw one new eligible card of each kind.

At Night School, you may keep your current cards or choose from 2 eligible
Career Cards and 2 Salary Cards.

The Police Officer collects $5,000 from any opponent who spins 10.

## Family, House, and LIFE Tiles

Get Married adds a spouse and awards one LIFE tile. Baby spaces add one child;
Twins adds two. Each of those family spaces awards one LIFE tile.

Buy a House requires a random House Deed and immediate payment. If you lack
cash, take enough $20,000 bank-loan notes to pay the price.

When instructed to take a LIFE tile, draw one face down. If the draw pile is
empty, take one unprotected tile from any opponent instead. Tile values remain
hidden until final scoring under the classic setting.

## Insurance

At the beginning of any turn, automobile insurance may be bought for $10,000.
It protects against automobile-loss spaces.

Homeowner's insurance may be bought when purchasing a house or at the
beginning of a later turn. Its premium is printed on the House Deed and it
protects against home-loss spaces.

The simplified and disabled insurance settings are house-rule variants.

## Stocks

At the beginning of a turn, you may buy one available Stock numbered 1 through
9 for $50,000. Whenever any player spins your number, collect $10,000 from the
bank.

You normally own one Stock. Stock Market Zooms awards one extra Stock free,
even when you already own one. Stock Market Slumps makes you return one Stock.
Stock number 10 does not exist; a spin of 10 triggers the Police Officer rule.

## Bank Loans

During any turn, take one or more $20,000 bank-loan notes. Repay each note for
$25,000, either during a turn or compulsorily at retirement.

Required payments automatically issue enough loan notes when cash is short.

## Retirement and Winning

At retirement:

- Repay every bank loan with $5,000 interest per note.
- Return Career, Salary, insurance, and House Deed cards.
- Keep Stocks and LIFE tiles.
- Choose Millionaire Estates or Countryside Acres.

Countryside Acres awards one LIFE tile and protects all of that player's tiles
from being taken.

Millionaire Estates awards no immediate tile and does not protect tiles. Once
everyone retires, the Estates retiree with the most cash receives the four
reserved LIFE tiles. Two tied richest players receive two each.

Finally, reveal LIFE tiles and add their values to cash. Highest total wins.

## Controls

- `R` or `Space`: spin
- `S`: your status
- `Shift+S`: detailed status for all players

Career, Salary, insurance, Stock, loan, LIFE-tile, and retirement decisions
appear as accessible menu actions.

## Lobby Variants

The 145-space route, College choice, family rules, hidden LIFE tiles, full
insurance, Stocks, classic retirement, fixed event layout, and $10,000
starting cash form the rules-accurate default.

Shorter tracks, revealed or disabled LIFE tiles, simplified or disabled
insurance, disabled Stocks, altered retirement, randomized events, disabled
College or family mechanics, and different starting cash are optional
PlayPalace house rules.
